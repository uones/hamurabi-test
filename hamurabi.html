HAMURABI

CREATIVE COMPUTING · MORRISTOWN, NEW JERSEY

Try your hand at governing ancient Sumeria for a ten-year term of office.

You must manage people, acres of land, and bushels of grain. Each person needs 20 bushels per year to survive. It takes ½ bushel to plant one acre; each person can work up to 10 acres. Land is traded in bushels; the price changes each year. Beware rats, plagues, and starvation—if more than 45% starve in one year, you are impeached.

About this game

Hamurabi started in 1968 as “The Sumer Game” (Doug Dyment, FOCAL on PDP-8). David H. Ahl ported it to BASIC and published it in 101 BASIC Computer Games (1973) and BASIC Computer Games (1978). It became a classic on 8-bit micros including the Atari. This version follows the 1978 BASIC rules.
